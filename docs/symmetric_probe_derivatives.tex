\begin{abstract}
Probing a Tucker tensor train (T3) evaluates its actions: contracting the tensor with a
collection of vectors in all modes but one. We derive the \emph{symmetric} directional
derivatives of this map --- the derivatives obtained by perturbing every input vector in the
\emph{same} direction $\mathbf P$, repeated $t$ times --- and show they are computed by a single
probing-style sweep that carries an extra Taylor-jet axis. The whole construction follows from one
Leibniz rule for jets: because each input vector enters affinely, its jet truncates at order one,
and every quantity built from it is assembled by a binomial convolution of jets. This note records
the math behind \texttt{t3toolbox/backend/sampling\_derivatives.py}. It treats the Euclidean (plain-T3)
and Riemannian (tangent-vector) probing maps, both forward and transpose, the latter by a
jet-ified adjoint-state Lagrangian. Section~\ref{sec:extensions} sketches the extensions that the code
also implements: the all-modes special cases (\emph{apply}/\emph{entries}), the full batch stacking
(including the tangent stack), and the corewise (non-manifold) transpose; the base-free \emph{ambient}
transpose is deferred (see the project note). This material is not part of the published T4S paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup: probing and its actions}
\label{sec:setup}

We recall just enough of the probing construction to fix notation; see the T4S paper for the full
development.

A \emph{Tucker tensor train} is a pair of core families $\mathbb T = \big((\mathbf U_i)_{i=1}^d,\,
(\mathbf G_i)_{i=1}^d\big)$, where each \emph{Tucker factor} $\mathbf U_i \in \R^{n_i \times N_i}$
has full row rank ($n_i \le N_i$) and each \emph{tensor-train core} $\mathbf G_i \in \R^{r_{i-1}
\times n_i \times r_i}$, with boundary ranks $r_0 = r_d = 1$. It represents a tensor $\mathbf T \in
\R^{N_1 \times \cdots \times N_d}$. For a vector $\mathbf v \in \R^{n_i}$ we write $\mathbf
G_i(\mathbf v) \in \R^{r_{i-1} \times r_i}$ for the matrix obtained by contracting the middle index,
\[
  \mathbf G_i(\mathbf v)[a,b] \;=\; \sum_{n} \mathbf G_i[a,n,b]\,\mathbf v[n].
\]

\paragraph{Actions (probing).}
Given input vectors $\mathbf W = (\mathbf w_1, \dots, \mathbf w_d)$ with $\mathbf w_i \in \R^{N_i}$,
the $i$th \emph{action} $\mathbf z_i$ is the result of contracting $\mathbf T$ with $\mathbf w_j$ in
every mode $j \ne i$, leaving mode $i$ free:
\[
  \mathbf z_i \;=\; T(\mathbf w_1, \dots, \mathbf w_{i-1}, \,\cdot\,, \mathbf w_{i+1}, \dots, \mathbf w_d)
  \;\in\; \R^{N_i}.
\]
Probing computes all $d$ actions in two sweeps over the train. Introduce the \emph{edge variables}
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol\xi_i      &= \mathbf U_i \mathbf w_i \in \R^{n_i},
  & &\text{(projected probe)} \label{eq:xi}\\
  \boldsymbol\mu_0^\top  &= 1, &
  \boldsymbol\mu_i^\top &= \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top\, \mathbf G_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i) \in \R^{r_i},
  & &\text{(left pushthrough)} \label{eq:mu}\\
  \boldsymbol\nu_d      &= 1, &
  \boldsymbol\nu_i      &= \mathbf G_{i+1}(\boldsymbol\xi_{i+1})\, \boldsymbol\nu_{i+1} \in \R^{r_i},
  & &\text{(right pushthrough)} \label{eq:nu}\\
  \boldsymbol\eta_i     &= \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top\, \mathbf G_i\, \boldsymbol\nu_i \in \R^{n_i},
  & &\text{(combine, mode $i$ free)} \label{eq:eta}\\
  \mathbf z_i           &= \mathbf U_i^\top \boldsymbol\eta_i \in \R^{N_i}.
  & &\text{(lift)} \label{eq:z}
\end{align}
Here $\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top \mathbf G_i \boldsymbol\nu_i$ denotes the vector obtained by
contracting $\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}$ and $\boldsymbol\nu_i$ into the left and right bond indices of
$\mathbf G_i$, leaving the middle index free. The single Tucker factor $\mathbf U_i$ is used twice:
to \emph{project} the probe \eqref{eq:xi} and to \emph{lift} the result \eqref{eq:z}.

\paragraph{Goal.}
Fix a direction $\mathbf P = (\mathbf p_1, \dots, \mathbf p_d)$, $\mathbf p_i \in \R^{N_i}$. We seek
the \emph{symmetric directional derivatives} of each action with respect to the inputs, taken $t$
times in the single repeated direction $\mathbf P$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:goal}
  \mathbf z_i^{(t)} \;:=\; \frac{\dd{^t}}{\dd s^t}\, \mathbf z_i(\mathbf W + s\mathbf P)\Big|_{s=0}
  \;=\; D^t \mathbf z_i(\mathbf W)\,\mathbf P^{t},
  \qquad t = 0, 1, \dots, m.
\end{equation}
Order $t=0$ is the ordinary probe. The point is that the \emph{same} direction is fed into all $t$
slots; this is what makes the whole family $t=0,\dots,m$ computable in a single sweep.

\section{Jets and the Leibniz rule}
\label{sec:jets}

The entire derivation rests on viewing every quantity as a function of the scalar $s$ along the ray
$\mathbf W + s\mathbf P$ and tracking its Taylor data.

\begin{definition}[Jet]
\label{def:jet}
Let $q$ be a quantity that depends smoothly on the inputs $\mathbf W$. Its \emph{order-$m$ jet}
(along $\mathbf P$, at $\mathbf W$) is the tuple of symmetric derivatives
\[
  \jet{q} \;=\; \big(q^{(0)}, q^{(1)}, \dots, q^{(m)}\big),
  \qquad
  q^{(t)} := \frac{\dd{^t}}{\dd s^t}\, q(\mathbf W + s\mathbf P)\Big|_{s=0}.
\]
So $q^{(0)} = q(\mathbf W)$ is the base value and $q^{(t)}$ is the quantity \eqref{eq:goal} sought
for $q = \mathbf z_i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Affine inputs have order-one jets]
\label{lem:affine}
Each probe vector is affine in $s$, $\mathbf w_i(s) = \mathbf w_i + s\,\mathbf p_i$, so
\[
  \jet{\mathbf w_i} = (\mathbf w_i,\ \mathbf p_i,\ 0, \dots, 0).
\]
Consequently the projected probe \eqref{eq:xi}, being a fixed linear image, also has an order-one
jet:
\[
  \jet{\boldsymbol\xi_i} = (\boldsymbol\xi_i,\ \delta\boldsymbol\xi_i,\ 0, \dots, 0),
  \qquad \delta\boldsymbol\xi_i := \mathbf U_i \mathbf p_i.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\mathbf w_i(s)$ is affine, so its second and higher $s$-derivatives vanish. The projection
$\boldsymbol\xi_i(s) = \mathbf U_i \mathbf w_i(s) = \mathbf U_i \mathbf w_i + s\,\mathbf U_i \mathbf
p_i$ is again affine.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Leibniz rule for jets]
\label{lem:leibniz}
Let $B : \R^{A} \times \R^{B} \to \R^{C}$ be a fixed bilinear map, and let $a(s), b(s)$ depend
smoothly on $s$. Then the jet of $s \mapsto B(a(s), b(s))$ is the binomial convolution of the jets of
$a$ and $b$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:leibniz}
  \big[B(a,b)\big]^{(t)} \;=\; \sum_{r=0}^{t} \binom{t}{r}\, B\!\big(a^{(r)},\, b^{(t-r)}\big),
  \qquad t = 0, 1, \dots, m.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By bilinearity, $\tfrac{\dd}{\dd s} B(a,b) = B(a',b) + B(a,b')$. This is the ordinary product rule
for the bilinear pairing $B$, so the general Leibniz rule for the $t$th derivative of a product gives
\eqref{eq:leibniz}; the binomial coefficients count how the $t$ differentiations are distributed
between the two arguments. Evaluating at $s=0$ replaces each derivative by the corresponding jet
component.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:leibniz} is the only engine we need. Every edge variable in
\eqref{eq:xi}--\eqref{eq:z} is built from the inputs by a chain of bilinear pairings (and one final
linear map), so its jet is obtained by applying \eqref{eq:leibniz} at each pairing. The two regimes
that occur differ only in how many jet components the second argument has: \emph{the input jet has
just two} (Lemma~\ref{lem:affine}), while \emph{a pushthrough jet has all $m{+}1$}.

\begin{remark}[Jets as a truncated algebra]
\label{rem:algebra}
Equivalently, identify $\jet{q}$ with the truncated Taylor polynomial $\sum_{t} \tfrac{1}{t!}
q^{(t)} s^{t} \pmod{s^{m+1}}$. Then \eqref{eq:leibniz} is exactly multiplication in the algebra
$\R[s]/(s^{m+1})$ (the Cauchy product, with the $\tfrac1{t!}$ normalisation absorbed into the
binomial weights). We keep the unnormalised derivatives $q^{(t)}$ rather than Taylor coefficients
because the binomial form maps directly onto integer-exact arithmetic in the implementation.
\end{remark}

\section{Symmetric derivatives of the actions}
\label{sec:derivs}

We now apply Lemma~\ref{lem:leibniz} to each edge-variable recursion. Throughout, the cores $\mathbf
U_i, \mathbf G_i$ are constants (independent of $s$), so the only $s$-dependence is carried by the
edge variables.

\begin{theorem}[Pushthrough jets]
\label{thm:push}
The left and right pushthrough jets satisfy, for $t = 1, \dots, m$,
\begin{align}
  \big(\boldsymbol\mu_i^{(t)}\big)^\top
    &= \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t)}\big)^\top \mathbf G_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i)
     \;+\; t\,\big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t-1)}\big)^\top \mathbf G_i(\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i),
    \label{eq:dmu}\\[2pt]
  \boldsymbol\nu_i^{(t)}
    &= \mathbf G_{i+1}(\boldsymbol\xi_{i+1})\, \boldsymbol\nu_{i+1}^{(t)}
     \;+\; t\,\mathbf G_{i+1}(\delta\boldsymbol\xi_{i+1})\, \boldsymbol\nu_{i+1}^{(t-1)},
    \label{eq:dnu}
\end{align}
with base values $\boldsymbol\mu_i^{(0)} = \boldsymbol\mu_i$, $\boldsymbol\nu_i^{(0)} =
\boldsymbol\nu_i$, and boundary jets $\boldsymbol\mu_0^{(0)} = 1$, $\boldsymbol\mu_0^{(t)} = 0$
($t>0$), $\boldsymbol\nu_d^{(0)} = 1$, $\boldsymbol\nu_d^{(t)} = 0$ ($t>0$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The recursion \eqref{eq:mu} reads $\boldsymbol\mu_i^\top = B(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1},
\boldsymbol\xi_i)$ with the bilinear map $B(\mathbf u, \mathbf v) = \mathbf u^\top \mathbf G_i(\mathbf
v)$ (linear in $\mathbf u$, and linear in $\mathbf v$ since $\mathbf G_i(\cdot)$ is). Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:leibniz}:
\[
  \big(\boldsymbol\mu_i^{(t)}\big)^\top
  = \sum_{r=0}^{t} \binom{t}{r}\, \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(r)}\big)^\top
      \mathbf G_i\!\big(\boldsymbol\xi_i^{(t-r)}\big).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:affine}, $\boldsymbol\xi_i^{(t-r)}$ is nonzero only for $t-r \in \{0,1\}$, i.e.\
$r \in \{t, t-1\}$, leaving the two terms of \eqref{eq:dmu} with weights $\binom{t}{t}=1$ and
$\binom{t}{t-1}=t$. The boundary jet $\jet{\boldsymbol\mu_0}$ is constant ($\boldsymbol\mu_0 \equiv
1$), so all its derivatives above order $0$ vanish. Equation \eqref{eq:dnu} is identical with the
train reversed.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:dmu} is a \emph{bidiagonal} recursion in the order index $t$: the order is
preserved when the core is fed the base projection $\boldsymbol\xi_i$ and raised by one (with weight
$t$) when it is fed the direction $\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i$. The bidiagonal (only two-term) shape is
exactly the statement that the input jet stops at order one.

\begin{theorem}[Action jets]
\label{thm:action}
For $i = 1, \dots, d$ and $t = 0, 1, \dots, m$,
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol\eta_i^{(t)}
    &= \sum_{r=0}^{t} \binom{t}{r}\,
       \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(r)}\big)^\top \mathbf G_i\, \boldsymbol\nu_i^{(t-r)},
    \label{eq:deta}\\[2pt]
  \mathbf z_i^{(t)} &= \mathbf U_i^\top \boldsymbol\eta_i^{(t)}.
    \label{eq:dz}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The combine \eqref{eq:eta} is $\boldsymbol\eta_i = B(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}, \boldsymbol\nu_i)$ with the
bilinear map $B(\mathbf u, \mathbf v) = \mathbf u^\top \mathbf G_i \mathbf v$ (mode $i$ free), so
\eqref{eq:deta} is Lemma~\ref{lem:leibniz} verbatim. Unlike the pushthrough, \emph{both} arguments
carry full jets, so the sum runs over all $r = 0, \dots, t$. Finally \eqref{eq:z} is the fixed
linear map $\boldsymbol\eta_i \mapsto \mathbf U_i^\top \boldsymbol\eta_i$, whose jet acts
componentwise: $\mathbf z_i^{(t)} = \mathbf U_i^\top \boldsymbol\eta_i^{(t)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The full sum, $r = 0$ to $t$]
\label{rem:fullsum}
The action sum \eqref{eq:deta} runs over the \emph{full} range $r = 0, \dots, t$; both endpoints
($\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(0)} = \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}$ paired with $\boldsymbol\nu_i^{(t)}$, and
$\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t)}$ paired with $\boldsymbol\nu_i^{(0)} = \boldsymbol\nu_i$) are genuine
product-rule terms. An earlier unpublished draft wrote this sum starting at $r=1$; that is an error.
The forms above are verified against a dense oracle (Section~\ref{sec:impl}).
\end{remark}

\section{The sweeping algorithm}
\label{sec:algo}

Theorems~\ref{thm:push}--\ref{thm:action} give an algorithm that produces the entire derivative
family $\{\mathbf z_i^{(t)}\}_{i,t}$ in two sweeps, exactly mirroring ordinary probing but carrying
the order index $t$.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Symmetric probe derivatives of a Tucker tensor train}
\label{alg:sweep}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require T3 $\big((\mathbf U_i),(\mathbf G_i)\big)$; inputs $\mathbf W$; direction $\mathbf P$; order $m$
\Ensure derivative jets $\jet{\mathbf z_i}$ for $i=1,\dots,d$
\For{$i = 1,\dots,d$} \Comment{project, forming the order-one input jets}
  \State $\boldsymbol\xi_i \gets \mathbf U_i \mathbf w_i$,\quad
         $\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i \gets \mathbf U_i \mathbf p_i$
\EndFor
\State $\jet{\boldsymbol\mu_0} \gets (1,0,\dots,0)$
\For{$i = 1,\dots,d$} \Comment{left sweep, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmu}}
  \State $\boldsymbol\mu_i^{(t)} \gets \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t)}\mathbf G_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i)
         + t\,\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t-1)}\mathbf G_i(\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i)$ \textbf{ for } $t=0,\dots,m$
\EndFor
\State $\jet{\boldsymbol\nu_d} \gets (1,0,\dots,0)$
\For{$i = d,\dots,1$} \Comment{right sweep, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dnu}}
  \State $\boldsymbol\nu_{i-1}^{(t)} \gets \mathbf G_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i)\boldsymbol\nu_i^{(t)}
         + t\,\mathbf G_i(\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i)\boldsymbol\nu_i^{(t-1)}$ \textbf{ for } $t=0,\dots,m$
\EndFor
\For{$i = 1,\dots,d$} \Comment{combine and lift, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:deta}--\eqref{eq:dz}}
  \State $\boldsymbol\eta_i^{(t)} \gets \sum_{r=0}^{t}\binom{t}{r}
         \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(r)}\big)^\top \mathbf G_i\,\boldsymbol\nu_i^{(t-r)}$ \textbf{ for } $t=0,\dots,m$
  \State $\mathbf z_i^{(t)} \gets \mathbf U_i^\top \boldsymbol\eta_i^{(t)}$ \textbf{ for } $t=0,\dots,m$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Cost.}
Relative to a single probe, the pushthrough sweeps carry an order axis of length $m+1$, each step
combining only the orders $t$ and $t-1$ (the input jet is order one), so they cost a factor $O(m)$
more. The combine \eqref{eq:deta} is a length-$(m{+}1)$ binomial convolution at each free mode, an
$O(m^2)$ factor over a single combine. There are no other changes: the algorithm is the ordinary
probing pipeline with the order axis threaded through.

\section{The unifying view and the implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

\paragraph{One operation.}
Theorems~\ref{thm:push} and \ref{thm:action} are the \emph{same} statement --- Lemma~\ref{lem:leibniz}
--- applied to two different bilinear pairings. Both are driven by the binomial tensor
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:trs}
  \texttt{trs}[t,r,s] \;=\; \binom{t}{r}\,[\,r + s = t\,],
\end{equation}
where $[\,\cdot\,]$ is the Iverson bracket. The pushthrough is the convolution of a pushthrough jet
(index $r$) with the input jet (index $s$, supported on $s \in \{0,1\}$), and the combine is the
convolution of the left and right pushthrough jets (indices $r$ and $s$, both full). In both cases
$r$ and $s$ are summed against $\texttt{trs}$ to produce the output order $t$. This is the product in
the truncated jet algebra of Remark~\ref{rem:algebra}.

\paragraph{Contractions.}
The implementation (\texttt{t3toolbox/backend/sampling\_derivatives.py}) realises each step as a
single grouped einsum, written as a subscript string in the dialect of
\texttt{backend.contractions.contract} (lowercase $t,r,s$ for the order axes, capital $W$ for the
sample stack and $C$ for the base stack --- each capital standing for a \emph{group} of zero or more
axes, as in plain probing):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
step & contraction & probing analogue \\
\midrule
pushthrough \eqref{eq:dmu}/\eqref{eq:dnu}
  & \texttt{'trs,rWCa,Caib,sWCi->tWCb'} & \texttt{'WCa,Caib,WCi->WCb'} \\
combine \eqref{eq:deta}
  & \texttt{'trs,rWCa,Caib,sWCb->tWCi'} & \texttt{'WCa,Caib,WCb->WCi'} \\
lift \eqref{eq:dz}
  & \texttt{'tWCi,Cio->tWCo'} & \texttt{'WCi,Cio->WCo'} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first two share the tensor \eqref{eq:trs}; for the pushthrough its $s$ axis is truncated to
$\{0,1\}$. The order axis $t$ is outermost. A flat \emph{sample stack} lets one call evaluate many
$(\mathbf W, \mathbf P)$ pairs at once: each sample is a paired input/direction, so to sweep many
directions at one point one repeats that point across the stack.

\paragraph{Verification.}
Each action is multilinear in the off-mode inputs, so $\mathbf z_i(\mathbf W + s\mathbf P)$ is a
polynomial in $s$ and the $t$th derivative is exact:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:oracle}
  \mathbf z_i^{(t)} \;=\; t!\!\!\sum_{\substack{S \subseteq \{1,\dots,d\}\setminus\{i\}\\ |S| = t}}
  T\big(\dots\big),
\end{equation}
the inner contraction feeding $\mathbf p_j$ in the modes $j \in S$ and $\mathbf w_j$ elsewhere. The
sweeping algorithm matches this dense subset-expansion oracle to machine precision in
\texttt{tests/\allowbreak test\_probe\_derivatives.py}, across orders, ranks, and sample-stack shapes.

\section{Riemannian: symmetric derivatives of tangent-vector probing}
\label{sec:riem}

The construction above probes a fixed tensor. In the Riemannian setting one instead probes a
\emph{tangent vector} of the T3 manifold and differentiates that probing map with respect to the
input vectors --- the operator needed to fit a tangent vector to measured derivative data.

\paragraph{Tangent-vector probing.}
A tangent vector $v$ at a base point is represented by an orthogonal frame
$\big((\mathbf U_i),(\mathbf O_i),(\mathbf P_i),(\mathbf Q_i)\big)$ (up-Tucker, down-, left-, and
right-TT cores) and a gauged variation $\delta\mathbb V = \big((\delta\mathbf U_i),
(\delta\mathbf G_i)\big)$; see Appendix~A of the T4S paper. Its $i$th action on $\mathbf W$ is computed
by a base sweep through the frame,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rbase}
  \boldsymbol\xi_i = \mathbf U_i \mathbf w_i, \quad
  \boldsymbol\mu_i^\top = \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top \mathbf P_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i), \quad
  \boldsymbol\nu_i = \mathbf Q_{i+1}(\boldsymbol\xi_{i+1})\,\boldsymbol\nu_{i+1}, \quad
  \boldsymbol\eta_i = \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top \mathbf O_i\, \boldsymbol\nu_i,
\end{equation}
(the plain pipeline \eqref{eq:xi}--\eqref{eq:z} with the frame cores $\mathbf P,\mathbf Q,\mathbf O$
in place of $\mathbf G$), and a variation sweep
\begin{align}
  \delta\boldsymbol\xi_i &= \delta\mathbf U_i \mathbf w_i, \label{eq:rdxi}\\
  \boldsymbol\sigma_i^\top &= \boldsymbol\sigma_{i-1}^\top \mathbf Q_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i)
    + \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top \delta\mathbf G_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i)
    + \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top \mathbf O_i(\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i), \label{eq:rsigma}\\
  \boldsymbol\tau_i &= \delta\mathbf G_{i+1}(\boldsymbol\xi_{i+1})\boldsymbol\nu_{i+1}
    + \mathbf O_{i+1}(\delta\boldsymbol\xi_{i+1})\boldsymbol\nu_{i+1}
    + \mathbf P_{i+1}(\boldsymbol\xi_{i+1})\boldsymbol\tau_{i+1}, \label{eq:rtau}\\
  \delta\boldsymbol\eta_i &= \boldsymbol\sigma_{i-1}^\top \mathbf Q_i \boldsymbol\nu_i
    + \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top \delta\mathbf G_i \boldsymbol\nu_i
    + \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^\top \mathbf P_i \boldsymbol\tau_i, \label{eq:rdeta}\\
  \mathbf z_i &= \mathbf U_i^\top \delta\boldsymbol\eta_i + \delta\mathbf U_i^\top \boldsymbol\eta_i,
    \label{eq:rz}
\end{align}
with $\boldsymbol\sigma_0 = 0$, $\boldsymbol\tau_d = 0$ (Algorithm~7 of the T4S paper).

\paragraph{Jets.}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:rbase}--\eqref{eq:rz} with respect to $\mathbf W$ along $\mathbf P$ uses
nothing new: every right-hand side is a chain of bilinear pairings, so Lemma~\ref{lem:leibniz}
applies term by term. The base sweep \eqref{eq:rbase} is verbatim Theorems~\ref{thm:push}--%
\ref{thm:action} with the frame cores. The variation sweep jet-ifies the same way; with the
order-one input jets $\jet{\boldsymbol\xi_i} = (\boldsymbol\xi_i, \mathbf U_i\mathbf p_i, 0, \dots)$
and $\jet{\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i} = (\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i, \delta\mathbf U_i\mathbf p_i, 0, \dots)$,
\eqref{eq:rsigma} becomes the bidiagonal recursion
\begin{align}
  \big(\boldsymbol\sigma_i^{(t)}\big)^\top
  &= \big(\boldsymbol\sigma_{i-1}^{(t)}\big)^\top \mathbf Q_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i)
   + t \big(\boldsymbol\sigma_{i-1}^{(t-1)}\big)^\top \mathbf Q_i(\mathbf U_i\mathbf p_i) \nonumber\\
  &\;+ \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t)}\big)^\top \delta\mathbf G_i(\boldsymbol\xi_i)
     + t \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t-1)}\big)^\top \delta\mathbf G_i(\mathbf U_i\mathbf p_i) \nonumber\\
  &\;+ \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t)}\big)^\top \mathbf O_i(\delta\boldsymbol\xi_i)
     + t \big(\boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(t-1)}\big)^\top \mathbf O_i(\delta\mathbf U_i\mathbf p_i),
  \label{eq:rdsigma}
\end{align}
and $\boldsymbol\tau$, $\delta\boldsymbol\eta$, $\mathbf z$ likewise (the combine \eqref{eq:rdeta} and
lift \eqref{eq:rz} being full binomial convolutions, as in \eqref{eq:deta}--\eqref{eq:dz}).

\begin{remark}[The order axis is uniform]
\label{rem:uniform}
Every edge variable above --- base \emph{and} variation --- depends on $\mathbf W$, so every one
carries the order axis. The forward Riemannian sweep therefore introduces \emph{no new contractions}:
each term of \eqref{eq:rdsigma} and its companions is one of the three jet contractions of
Section~\ref{sec:impl}, fed a frame core ($\mathbf P,\mathbf Q,\mathbf O$) or the variation core
$\delta\mathbf G$. (Contrast the manifold tangent stack of many tangents at one base, which rides
only the variation-derived edge variables and so needs asymmetric three-block contractions.)
\end{remark}

\section{The transpose, by a jet-ified adjoint Lagrangian}
\label{sec:transpose}

The map $\delta\mathbb V \mapsto \{\mathbf z_i^{(t)}\}$ of Section~\ref{sec:riem} is \emph{linear} in
the variation. Fitting needs its transpose: given residual jets $\mathbf r = \{\mathbf r_i^{(t)}\}$ in
the action-jet space, return the variation gradient $\big((\delta\widetilde{\mathbf U}_i),
(\delta\widetilde{\mathbf G}_i)\big)$. We obtain it by the adjoint-state method, jet-ified.

\paragraph{The Lagrangian.}
Introduce an adjoint (multiplier) jet for each forward edge-variable equation
\eqref{eq:rdxi}--\eqref{eq:rdeta} --- $\widehat{\delta\boldsymbol\eta}_i,\ \widehat{\boldsymbol\sigma}_i,\
\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}_i,\ \widehat{\delta\boldsymbol\xi}_i$ --- and form
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:lagr}
  \mathcal L = \big\langle \mathbf r,\ \mathbf z \big\rangle
    + \big\langle \widehat\Xi,\ E(\Xi) \big\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ sums over modes \emph{and the order axis}, $\mathbf z$ is
\eqref{eq:rz}, and $E(\Xi)$ collects the residuals of the forward equations. This is Theorem~7 of the
T4S paper with one change: \emph{every contraction in $\mathcal L$ is replaced by its $\texttt{trs}$
version}, so each product of edge variables becomes the binomial jet convolution of
Section~\ref{sec:impl}. At a stationary point the constraints hold ($\partial_{\widehat\Xi}\mathcal L
= 0$ recovers the forward jets), the adjoint equations define the multiplier jets
($\partial_\Xi\mathcal L = 0$), and
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:grad}
  \big((\delta\widetilde{\mathbf U}_i),(\delta\widetilde{\mathbf G}_i)\big)
  = \partial_{\delta\mathbb V}\, \mathcal L = \big(J^{(s)}\big)^\top(\mathbf r).
\end{equation}
Because the constraints are the jet-ified forward equations, both the adjoint sweeps and the gradient
are read off by differentiating $\texttt{trs}$ contractions, with two clean consequences.

\paragraph{Adjoint sweeps: the same tensor, transposed.}
The multiplier equations $\partial_\Xi\mathcal L = 0$ are the adjoint sweeps. Differentiating a forward
jet convolution with respect to one of its operands \emph{transposes} the $\texttt{trs}$ contraction:
the output order and a summed input order swap roles. For the pushthrough and its adjoint (with respect
to the swept edge variable $\mathbf a$),
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:hook}
  \underbrace{\texttt{trs}[t,r,s]\, \mathbf a^{(r)} \mathbf G\, \boldsymbol\xi^{(s)} \to (\cdot)^{(t)}}
  _{\text{forward, output order }t}
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  \underbrace{\texttt{trs}[t,r,s]\, \mathbf G\, \boldsymbol\xi^{(s)}\, \widetilde{(\cdot)}^{(t)} \to \mathbf a^{(r)}}
  _{\text{adjoint, output order }r\ (t\text{ summed})}.
\end{equation}
It is literally the same kernel \eqref{eq:trs} wired as its transpose --- the adjoint of a binomial
convolution is a binomial correlation. Thus $\widehat{\boldsymbol\sigma},\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau},
\widehat{\delta\boldsymbol\xi}$ are computed by the adjoint-hooked analogues of
\eqref{eq:rsigma}--\eqref{eq:rdeta}, in the reverse sweep direction, while $\widehat{\delta\boldsymbol
\eta}_i = \mathbf U_i \mathbf r_i$ is the order-diagonal lift.

\paragraph{Gradient assembly: arity equals internal-edge count.}
The variation gradients $\partial_{\delta\mathbf G_i}\mathcal L$ and $\partial_{\delta\mathbf U_i}
\mathcal L$ are \emph{order-less} (the variation cores carry no order axis). Differentiating the
Lagrangian's contractions with respect to a core sums out the order axes, and \emph{the number summed
equals the number of internal edges of that core}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the TT core $\delta\mathbf G_i$ has three internal edges (its left and right bonds and the
    up-edge to its Tucker core), so its gradient contracts three jets against the three-leg tensor
    $\texttt{trs}$. It is the sum of the core-adjoints of the three $\delta\mathbf G$-terms in
    \eqref{eq:rsigma}, \eqref{eq:rtau}, \eqref{eq:rdeta} --- each an order-less three-fold outer
    product placed on the (left-bond, mode, right-bond) legs of the core --- e.g.\ from
    \eqref{eq:rsigma},
    \[
      \delta\widetilde{\mathbf G}_i \;\supset\; \sum_{r+s=t}\texttt{trs}[t,r,s]\
        \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}^{(r)} \otimes \boldsymbol\xi_i^{(s)} \otimes \widehat{\boldsymbol\sigma}_i^{(t)};
    \]
  \item the Tucker core $\delta\mathbf U_i$ has one internal edge (the down-edge to its TT core), so
    its gradient contracts against the one-leg ``tensor'' --- a plain sum over the single order axis:
    \[
      \delta\widetilde{\mathbf U}_i
      = \sum_t \boldsymbol\eta_i^{(t)} \otimes \mathbf r_i^{(t)}
      + \sum_u \widehat{\delta\boldsymbol\xi}_i^{(u)} \otimes \mathbf w_i^{(u)},
      \qquad \jet{\mathbf w_i} = (\mathbf w_i, \mathbf p_i, 0, \dots).
    \]
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[General networks]
\label{rem:arity}
The rule ``arity of the binomial assembly tensor $=$ number of internal edges of the assembled core''
is network-agnostic. A hierarchical-Tucker node with four internal edges would assemble against a
four-leg tensor $\texttt{trsq}$ (a multinomial convolution); the one- and three-edge cases above are
the trivial ($t$) and binomial ($\texttt{trs}$) instances.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Verification.}
To machine precision the transpose matches both the dense adjoint identity $\langle \mathbf r,
J^{(s)} v\rangle = \langle (J^{(s)})^\top \mathbf r, v\rangle$ and the forward map's automatic
transpose (\texttt{jax.\allowbreak linear\_\allowbreak transpose}), across orders and sample-stack
shapes.

\section{Extensions (implemented in the code)}
\label{sec:extensions}

The sections above derive probe derivatives, forward and transpose. The code
(\texttt{sampling\_derivatives.py}) also implements the following, each by the same jet calculus; we sketch
the math here and refer to \texttt{dev/archive/derivatives\_mirror\_plan.md} for the build details.

\paragraph{All-modes special cases: \emph{apply} and \emph{entries}.}
\emph{apply} contracts \emph{every} mode (probing leaves one free); with no free mode the pipeline
collapses to a single left-to-right pass --- the perturbation sweep $\boldsymbol\sigma$ kept only to its
\emph{terminal} carry and contracted at the boundary bond (no right/central sweeps, no per-mode
assembly). Its forward jet is therefore the terminal $\boldsymbol\sigma$-jet. Its transpose is the
adjoint-state Lagrangian with the per-mode residual gone: the scalar residual jet $\mathbf c$ \emph{seeds}
one propagation sweep at the terminal bond (a propagation-only $\widehat{\boldsymbol\sigma}$ sweep, no
$\widehat{\delta\boldsymbol\eta}$ source), and the gradient is
$\delta\widetilde{\mathbf G}_i = \boldsymbol\mu_{i-1}\otimes\boldsymbol\xi_i\otimes\widehat{\boldsymbol
\sigma}_i$, $\delta\widetilde{\mathbf U}_i = \widehat{\delta\boldsymbol\xi}_i\otimes\mathbf w_i$ (about
half the work of the probe transpose). \emph{entries} is \emph{apply} with one-hot inputs $\mathbf
e_{\mathrm{index}}$ in the base slot and a general direction $\mathbf P$ (fiber-slice the base projection,
contract $\mathbf P$ at order one); its transpose scatters onto the indexed rows.

\paragraph{Full batch stacking, including the tangent stack $K$.}
Three independent batch blocks thread orthogonally to the convolved order axis (which is outermost):
the sample stack $W$ (the paired $(\mathbf X,\mathbf P)$ samples, on the inputs), the tangent stack $K$
(a batch of tangent vectors sharing one base, on the variation cores), and the base stack $C$ (on the
cores), base-inner $W{+}K{+}C$. The base edge-variable jets carry $W{+}C$; the variation-derived jets
($\boldsymbol\sigma,\boldsymbol\tau,\delta\boldsymbol\eta,\delta\boldsymbol\xi$ and their adjoints) carry
$K$. Each forward/adjoint contraction becomes its \emph{order-threaded three-group} $(W,K,C)$ version
(the binomial tensor \eqref{eq:trs} riding on top), mirroring ordinary probing's $K$-stacked sweeps;
the split is recovered from operand shapes. Each reduces to the two-group form when $K=\varnothing$.

\paragraph{Corewise (non-manifold) transpose.}
The gradient of the plain-T3 derivative sampling map with respect to the cores as independent variables
(for non-manifold optimizers) is the Section-6.3 \emph{corewise simplification}: the tangent derivative
transpose with the base's own cores substituted for the orthogonal frames
($\mathbf P,\mathbf Q,\mathbf O\to\mathbf G_i$, $\mathbf U$ no longer required orthogonal). It is a
trivial substitution wrapper; verified against \texttt{jax.grad} of the forward.

\paragraph{Deferred: the ambient transpose.}
The base-free adjoint on the full tensor space (CP factors) is deferred: for the derivative map the
back-projection is inherently exponential-rank --- $[s^t]\!\prod_j(\mathbf w_j+s\mathbf p_j)$ is an
elementary-symmetric combination of rank $\binom{d}{t}$ --- unlike the clean rank-$1$/rank-$d$
non-derivative ambient. See \texttt{docs/ambient\_derivative\_transpose\_note.md}.

\section{Recurrence and convolution realization}
\label{sec:recurrence}

Each contraction of Section~\ref{sec:impl} sums the binomial tensor \eqref{eq:trs} against jets to
produce one output order. Forming \texttt{trs} as a dense einsum operand is wasteful --- it is a sparse
convolution tensor, and the einsum path treats it as dense, materialising an intermediate scaling with
the product of every index. Abstracting away the spatial cores and the batch stacks, every step is one
of two scalar shapes in the jet indices,
\begin{align}
  \text{(forward)} \qquad & c^{(t)} \;=\; \sum_{r+s=t} \binom{t}{r}\, a^{(r)} b^{(s)},
  \label{eq:conv-fwd}\\
  \text{(reverse)} \qquad & c^{(r)} \;=\; \sum_{t \ge r} \binom{t}{r}\, b^{(t-r)} z^{(t)},
  \label{eq:conv-rev}
\end{align}
where the reverse \eqref{eq:conv-rev} is the adjoint of the forward \eqref{eq:conv-fwd} in the argument
$a$: it equals $\partial/\partial a^{(r)}$ of the scalar $\sum_t z^{(t)} c^{(t)}$, and it is what
produces the transpose (tilde) edge variables. Each occurs in two regimes.

\paragraph{Affine input: two-term recurrences.}
When one factor is an \emph{input} jet it is supported on orders $\{0,1\}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:affine}), so
\eqref{eq:conv-fwd} and \eqref{eq:conv-rev} collapse to bidiagonal recurrences with no \texttt{trs}
tensor:
\begin{align}
  \text{forward, } b \text{ affine:} \qquad & c^{(t)} \;=\; a^{(t)} b^{(0)} + t\, a^{(t-1)} b^{(1)},
  \label{eq:rec-fwd}\\
  \text{reverse, } b \text{ affine:} \qquad & c^{(r)} \;=\; b^{(0)} z^{(r)} + (r+1)\, b^{(1)} z^{(r+1)}.
  \label{eq:rec-rev}
\end{align}
The reverse recurrence \eqref{eq:rec-rev} shifts \emph{up} ($z^{(r+1)}$, weight $r{+}1$) where the
forward \eqref{eq:rec-fwd} shifts \emph{down} ($a^{(t-1)}$, weight $t$): the transpose of a bidiagonal
map is bidiagonal. This drives the pushthroughs $\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\nu$
\eqref{eq:dmu}/\eqref{eq:dnu}, their variation analogues $\boldsymbol\sigma,\boldsymbol\tau$
\eqref{eq:rsigma}/\eqref{eq:rtau}, and the adjoint-propagation term of $\widetilde{\boldsymbol\sigma},
\widetilde{\boldsymbol\tau}$.

\paragraph{Full jets: convolution and correlation.}
When both factors are pushthrough jets --- the combines $\boldsymbol\eta,\delta\boldsymbol\eta$
\eqref{eq:deta} and the adjoint source of $\widetilde{\boldsymbol\sigma},\widetilde{\boldsymbol\tau},
\delta\widetilde{\boldsymbol\xi}$ --- neither is affine, and \eqref{eq:conv-fwd}/\eqref{eq:conv-rev} are
a genuine binomial convolution / correlation. Scaling to exponential-generating-function coordinates
$\hat a^{(r)} = a^{(r)}/r!$, $\hat b^{(s)} = b^{(s)}/s!$, $\hat z^{(t)} = t!\,z^{(t)}$ removes the
binomial weight and exhibits them as an ordinary convolution and its adjoint correlation:
\begin{equation}
  \hat c^{(t)} \;=\; \sum_{r+s=t} \hat a^{(r)} \hat b^{(s)}, \qquad
  r!\,c^{(r)} \;=\; \sum_{s \ge 0} \hat b^{(s)} \hat z^{(r+s)} .
  \label{eq:egf}
\end{equation}
Both are $O(m^2)$ in the order and admit no cheaper closed form --- a cumulative-sum shortcut linearises
the correlation only when the kernel $\hat b$ is constant, which is degenerate. Since $m \le d$ this is
negligible; the binding cost is memory.

\paragraph{Memory: scan the summed axes.}
The dense \texttt{trs} contraction holds a spatial intermediate for \emph{every} order at once. As the
order axis is only summed, each operation is instead realised as a \emph{scan} over an input order: the
convolution \eqref{eq:conv-fwd} scans $r$, forming one order-slice of the spatial product and folding in
the second jet in a single contraction; the correlation \eqref{eq:conv-rev} scans the full jet's order
likewise. One order slice is resident --- an $(m{+}1)\times$ reduction --- and it composes with the
outer core sweep (also a scan). On the packed (uniform) backend these dispatch to
\texttt{jax.lax.scan}/\texttt{lax.map}, which \emph{force} the sequential freeing; an unrolled loop lets
the compiler keep every slice live, so the scan primitive is essential rather than cosmetic.

\paragraph{The variation assembly.}
The transpose closes by assembling the variation-core gradients $\delta\widetilde{\mathbf G}_i,
\delta\widetilde{\mathbf U}_i$: order-summed outer products of the tilde edge variables with the base
jets. Here the order axes are \emph{fully} summed (no output order) and the free legs are a whole core
$(\mathrm{r_L},\mathrm{n},\mathrm{r_R})$, so the intermediate is the largest in the chain --- and it is
exactly \emph{linear} in the sample stack $W$. It is therefore chunked over $W$: the dense assembly runs
per $W$-block, and the blocks are combined by \emph{addition} when $W$ is summed (the minibatch
gradient, $\sum_{\text{probes}}$) or by \emph{concatenation} when $W$ is kept. The reducer is the only
axis-role-dependent piece, so the same seam chunks the base stack $C$ (always kept, hence concatenated)
when it, rather than $W$, is the large axis.
