\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Batched GPU/\code{jit} pipelines store matrices of varying sizes zero-padded to a common static
shape, with boolean masks recording which rows and columns are real. A black-box SVD of such a
padded matrix is \emph{wrong} whenever the real block is numerically rank-deficient: the
$\sigma\approx 0$ left singular vectors are an arbitrary orthonormal basis of a degenerate subspace
that \emph{contains the pad coordinates}, so they generically place mass on padded rows --- and a
downstream mask then erases them, silently losing a basis direction. In T3Toolbox this destroyed
the uniform-layer tangent frame exactly at the rank-continuation warm start, the point the library's
deflation strategy is built around. We surveyed the literature and found no batched, tolerance-free
treatment: every rank-adaptive method either builds its new directions explicitly (outside the SVD)
or pads and never asks the padded slots for orthonormality. These notes define the problem and the
output contract, describe the shipped algorithm --- \emph{sketch--project} (``Method~D''): a fixed
orthonormal sketch, a QR with pads permuted to the trailing pivots, one small augmented SVD, and a
right factor rebuilt from $\Apad^{\top}U = V\Sigma$ --- which realizes the contract exactly,
bitwise on the padding, with \emph{no rank tolerance anywhere}, at cost $O(NM^2+M^3)$ independent
of the pad count, in one \code{jit} compilation for all mask patterns. An appendix records the
alternatives we designed, measured, and rejected, including the two-sided augmentation and its
equivalence with the generalized SVD, post-hoc completion, masked noise, and the measurements that
settled the separation constant.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Setting}

Let $A\in\R^{n\times m}$ be embedded in a fixed-shape padded matrix
\[
  \Apad\in\R^{N\times M},\qquad N = n + P_r,\quad M = m + P_c,
\]
where $P_r$ row positions and $P_c$ column positions --- at \emph{arbitrary, possibly interior}
locations --- are identically zero. Boolean masks of static length record the partition; in a
batched setting the masks are runtime data and differ per batch element, while all array shapes are
static (the requirement for a single \code{jit}/\code{vmap}/\code{scan} compilation).

In T3Toolbox this is the \emph{uniform} representation of Tucker tensor trains: supercores padded to
common edge widths with prefix rank masks, so that a whole stack of tensors of varying ranks flows
through one compiled program \cite{t3docs}. Every orthogonalization step of the uniform sweeps ---
the Tucker down-orthogonalization, both TT sweeps, the down step, and the T3-SVD scan --- is an SVD
of a zero-padded unfolding, and the row index of an unfolding is often a Kronecker product of two
padded edges, which is how \emph{interior} padding arises even though each edge is padded only as a
suffix.

\subsection{The failure mode}\label{sec:failure}

If $A$ has numerical rank $k<\min(n,m)$, the left null space of $\Apad$ contains both the genuine
null directions of $A$ and the pad coordinates $\operatorname{span}\{e_i : i \text{ padded}\}$.
These are \emph{one} degenerate eigenspace of $\Apad\Apad^{\top}$; a black-box SVD returns an
arbitrary orthonormal basis of it, so the $\sigma\approx 0$ columns of $U$ generically place mass on
pad rows. Restricted to the real rows --- which is what the masks do downstream --- $U$ is then
non-orthonormal and possibly rank-deficient: \textbf{a silently lost basis direction}.

A useful minimal model (the ``$k/z/p$ model''): the rows of an unfolding are $k$ nonzero rows, $z$
real-but-numerically-zero rows, and $p$ padded rows. The first $r=\rank(A)$ left singular vectors
are determined by the data and automatically live in the $k$ rows ($u = Av/\sigma$, and padded rows
of $Av$ are bitwise zero). The remaining vectors are \emph{any} orthonormal basis of the
complement, which spans $\R^{k+z}$ in the unpadded problem but $\R^{k+z}\oplus\R^{p}$ in the padded
one. No factorization can keep them out of the $p$ rows without being told the partition --- an
adversary can swap a $z$ row for a $p$ row without changing the matrix --- so \emph{the masks must
enter the computation as data}. In floating point a roundoff-level $\sigma$ is as unconstrained as
an exact zero, so thresholding does not evade the problem, it only moves it.

Two facts sharpened the diagnosis in T3Toolbox. First, the truncating T3-SVD is \emph{immune}: its
completion columns are multiplied by $\sigma=0$ on their way into the neighboring core, so the
represented tensor is exact no matter where the completion lives (measured 60/60). The
\emph{frame} is not: its basis columns are the output. Second, the case that triggers the failure
is precisely the library's selling point --- the zero-padded \code{resize} warm start of rank
continuation, which is structurally minimal but numerically deficient in both edge families. A
padded restart has almost no gradient energy in the already-resolved eigenspace, which deflates the
Newton--CG system; jitter would re-introduce it. Before the fix, that warm start lost tangent
directions in 60/60 trials and the loss cascaded through the sweep.

\subsection{Prior art}\label{sec:prior}

We looked for existing treatments (survey 2026-08-23; sources with links in the design record's
\code{README}, under \code{repros/S1b/}). Nobody relies on the SVD's null-space completion to grow
a basis:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{TeNPy} \code{MPS.enlarge\_chi}: zero-fills one bond; for the other, draws random rows,
Gram--Schmidt-projects them against the existing ones, QR-orthonormalizes, and concatenates ---
explicit masked completion by hand, inside the real dimensions by construction.
\item The \textbf{rank-adaptive BUG integrator} \cite{CerutiKuschLubich2022} augments $(K(t_1),U_0)$
and takes an orthonormal basis of its range by QR, noting the result is independent of the basis
choice (the basis is gauge) and robust to small singular values.
\item \textbf{Riemannian rank-adaptive TT completion} \cite{VermeylenVandereyckenAbsil2024} never
pads: the rank increase is a step along the \emph{normal} part of the tangent cone, appended as new
core columns --- which is what a zero-padded warm start followed by one gradient step computes
implicitly, and why the padded restart works.
\item \textbf{DMRG subspace expansion} \cite{Hubig2015} appends a residual-derived term and lets the
next SVD restore orthonormality; the literature contrasts this \emph{structured} enrichment with
White's density-matrix \emph{noise}.
\item \textbf{Vendors}: PyTorch's \code{linalg.svd} documentation states outright that trailing
singular vectors ``can be arbitrary bases of the corresponding subspaces''; cuSOLVER's batched
kernels require equal sizes and say nothing about padded semantics; TensorLy's \code{pad\_tt\_rank}
zero-pads ``without changing the reconstruction'' and is silent on orthogonality; JAX's ragged-data
guidance is pad-and-mask, nothing SVD-specific.
\end{itemize}

None of these is batched, static-shape, and tolerance-free at once, which is what a \code{jit}-compiled
sweep needs. That gap is what the algorithm below fills.

\subsection{The output contract}\label{sec:contract}

\begin{definition}[Pad-safe SVD]\label{def:contract}
Given $\Apad\in\R^{N\times M}$ with boolean masks marking $n$ real rows and $m$ real columns
(padded entries bitwise zero), a \emph{pad-safe SVD} returns $U\in\R^{N\times K}$,
$s\in\R^{K}$, $V^{\top}\in\R^{K\times M}$ with $K=\min(N,M)$ such that, with $q=\min(n,m)$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item $\Apad = U\,\diag(s)\,V^{\top}$ --- a genuine economy SVD of the padded matrix;
\item the first $q$ triplets are a valid economy SVD of the unpadded real block $A$: its positive
singular values, then real-supported null completions, with $U[:,{:}q]$ \textbf{bitwise} zero on
padded rows and $V[:,{:}q]$ \textbf{bitwise} zero on padded columns (exact $=0.0$, not small);
\item the remaining $K-q$ don't-care triplets carry $s=0$ exactly;
\item \textbf{no rank tolerance appears anywhere}: every count comes from the masks or from
bitwise $\{0,1\}$ indicators, so exact and roundoff-level zeros need no distinction.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently: $\code{pad}\to\code{svd}\to\code{unpad} \;\equiv\; \code{svd}$ of the unpadded $A$.
\end{definition}

Two remarks on the contract. \emph{Symmetry:} it is stated in $q=\min(n,m)$, so any real $(n,m)$
--- tall, wide, or mixed within a batch --- is supported with no feasibility condition; this
matters because real counts are traced per-element data, so a runtime ``transpose if wide'' branch
is not expressible in one batched program (Appendix~\ref{app:sym}). \emph{Freedom:} a valid
economy SVD of $A$ constrains its null columns only in support and orthonormality, never in
direction; the completion is gauge. Demanding instead that \emph{all} $M$ columns of $U$ avoid the
padding would require $n\ge M$ and is both unnecessary and generally unachievable.

\section{The algorithm: sketch--project}\label{sec:alg}

The shipped algorithm (``Method~D'' in the design record) rests on one observation:

\begin{lemma}[The matvec principle]\label{lem:matvec}
$\Apad x$ has bitwise-zero padded entries for every $x$, and $\Apad^{\top}y$ ignores the padded
rows of $y$. Hence any method that touches $\Apad$ only through products generates left vectors in
$\col(\Apad)\perp\{e_i: i\text{ padded}\}$ by construction.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The permutation rule}\label{sec:perm}

The second ingredient makes a Householder QR respect the padding \emph{exactly}:

\begin{lemma}[Permutation rule]\label{lem:perm}
Permute the padded rows to the trailing pivot positions before a Householder QR. Then every
reflector is bitwise zero on the padded rows, hence so is every data-supported column of $Q$; and
once the real rows are exhausted the trailing submatrix is bitwise zero, so the remaining
reflectors are the identity and the surplus columns of $Q$ are \emph{exact} pad coordinate
vectors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The reflector at step $i$ is $v = x \mp \norm{x}e_i$ with $x$ the current column below the
diagonal. Entries of $v$ at zero rows of $x$ vanish \emph{except at the pivot position $i$}; with
pads trailing, the first $\min(n,M)$ pivots are real rows, so $v$ is zero on every padded row, and
$Hy = y - 2v(v^{\top}y)/(v^{\top}v)$ preserves bitwise zeros there ($0 - 0\cdot x = 0$). After the
real rows are exhausted the trailing block consists only of (bitwise-zero) padded rows, so LAPACK's
\code{larfg} produces $\tau = 0$, $H = I$, and $Q$'s surplus columns are
$H_1\cdots H_n e_j = e_j$, a padded coordinate --- flagged exactly by the bitwise indicator
$t_j = \max_i |Q_{ij}|\,[i\text{ padded}] \in \{0,1\}$.
\end{proof}

Without the permutation the failure is deterministic, not probabilistic: an in-place padded row can
become a pivot, and then either the trailing block is exactly zero and $H=I$ emits
$q = e_{\mathrm{pad}}$ --- a null column sitting $100\%$ on a pad --- or the trailing block is
$\varepsilon$-level roundoff and the normalized reflector places an $O(1)$ entry at the pad
coordinate. The worked example in the design record (\S\ref{app:blind}) also shows why no
\emph{later} repair is possible: the projected core $B = Q^{\top}\Apad$ is bit-for-bit identical
for a clean and a poisoned $Q$, so the contamination is invisible to everything downstream of the
QR. Prevention at $O(NM)$ beats any repair.

\subsection{The four steps}\label{sec:steps}

Fixed setup, once per width $M$: a Haar-orthonormal sketch $\Omega\in\R^{M\times M}$ (QR of a
fixed-seed Gaussian with sign fix; $\kappa(\Omega)=1$ avoids the mildly heavy-tailed
$\sigma_{\min}$ of a square Gaussian). $\Omega$ is drawn once and cached: per-call randomness buys
nothing, and a fixed sketch makes the whole algorithm deterministic. If $N<M$ (a statically wide
padded shape --- known at trace time), transpose, run, and swap the factors.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.},leftmargin=2.1cm,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Left basis.} $Y = \Apad\Omega$ (padded rows bitwise zero for any $\Omega$;
$\col(Y) = \col(\Apad)$ exactly since $\Omega$ is invertible --- randomized SVD with the
approximation removed, hence no oversampling parameter). Permute pads to the bottom, economy QR,
un-permute $\to Q$; compute the surplus indicator $t$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:perm}).
\item \textbf{One augmented SVD.} Form the statically shaped $M\times 2M$ matrix
$[\,B \;\; c\,\diag(t)\,]$ with $B = Q^{\top}\Apad$ and $c = 4\norm{\Apad}_F$
(\S\ref{sec:c}); take its economy SVD $W_1\Sigma_a(\cdot)$, \emph{discarding the right factor}.
Where $t_i=1$ the diagonal block pins the surplus coordinate at $\sigma=c$ as an \emph{exact}
triplet (row $i$ of $B$ is bitwise zero); where $t_i=0$ it contributes a zero column, harmless
because zero columns pollute only the discarded factor. The left Gram
$BB^{\top} + c^2\diag(t)$ is \emph{bitwise block-diagonal} between surplus and data coordinates, so
every kept left vector --- including the $\sigma=0$ completions, which are the whole point ---
avoids the surplus coordinates exactly in exact arithmetic, and to $O(\varepsilon)$ in floating
point.
\item \textbf{Classify, sort, assemble.} $\sigma > c/2$ is a $\{$pin, data$\}$ classifier with
$\norm{A}$-sized margins on both sides --- \emph{counting, not thresholding}. Order kept triplets
descending with pins last (one \code{argsort} of $\mathrm{where}(\sigma>c/2,\,-1,\,\sigma)$); zero
the (exact-arithmetic-zero) surplus coordinates of the kept columns of $W_1$; set $U = QW_1$ and
zero $s$ \emph{by the pin flag}, then beyond $q$. The first $q$ columns of $U$ are bitwise zero on
padded rows. Zeroing pins by flag rather than by position is what makes the contract symmetric in
$\min(n,m)$: for $n<m$ the pins land inside the first $m$ slots and positional zeroing would leak
$\sigma=c$ into the spectrum (Appendix~\ref{app:sym}).
\item \textbf{Rebuild $V$ from $U$.} $W = \Apad^{\top}U$ equals $V\Sigma$ \emph{exactly}, with
$\Sigma$ decreasing, and every column bitwise supported on real columns (padded columns of $\Apad$
kill those rows). Orthonormal-times-decreasing-diagonal is its own QR factorization up to column
signs, recovered from $\mathrm{sign}(\diag R)$: the permuted QR of $W$ (padded columns to the
trailing pivots) therefore \emph{is} $V$ --- leading columns the true right vectors, exactly, even
through repeated-$\sigma$ clusters; middle columns real-supported null completions; trailing
columns exact pad coordinates. The whole final $V$, in order, tolerance-free.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Counting]\label{prop:count}
The augmented SVD returns $M$ triplets, of which $p_\ell = \max(0, M-n)$ are pinned; the
$\min(n,M)$ survivors, sorted descending, contain the economy SVD of the real block in their first
$q=\min(n,m)$ slots: $\rank(A)$ positive values followed by exact-arithmetic zeros whose left
vectors fill the real left-null space. No tolerance decides any count: $m$ and $n$ come from the
masks, $p_\ell$ from the bitwise indicator $t$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The separation constant}\label{sec:c}

The constant $c$ is bounded on both sides, and both bounds were \emph{measured}
(\code{s1b\_c\_study.py} in the design record):

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{From below (fragile):} the natural-looking $c = 2\norm{\Apad}_2$ puts the threshold
$c/2$ exactly at $\sigma_{\max}$. The classifier compares singular values computed by two
\emph{different} factorizations (the augmented core's SVD and the norm), so whenever rounding lands
the augmented $\sigma_{\max}$ one ulp above the separately computed norm, \emph{the largest triplet
is classified as a pin and silently deleted}: measured on \textbf{152 of 400} generic rank-one
matrices. Treacherously, exactly-representable test data is saved by the strict inequality, so the
bug survives casual testing. $c = 4\norm{\Apad}_F$ gives multiplicative margins $\ge 2$ on both
sides: 0/400.
\item \textbf{From above (a refuted worry):} one might fear a large $c$ imposes an
$\varepsilon c$ backward-error floor on the kept singular values (the augmented core has norm
$\sim c$). Measured: the error stays at $\sim 10^{-16}$ even at $c = 10^{12}\norm{A}_F$, pins
present or not. The pins occupy \emph{bitwise-zero rows} of $B$, so the augmented core is bitwise
block-diagonal and Householder bidiagonalization keeps the blocks separated: the data block's
effective backward error is $\varepsilon\norm{B}$, not $\varepsilon c$. The same zero-preservation
that powers the algorithm firewalls its accuracy. The only genuine upper constraint is dtype
overflow (relevant in \code{float32}).
\end{itemize}

Guard $c = 4\norm{\Apad}_F + [\norm{\Apad}_F = 0]$ so the all-zero matrix is handled; use the
Frobenius norm (the spectral norm costs an SVD and buys nothing).

\subsection{Cost, batching, precision}\label{sec:cost}

Everything is $N\times M$, $M\times 2M$, or $M\times M$; no $N\times N$ object exists. Cost
$O(NM^2 + M^3)$, \emph{independent of the pad counts} --- the big matrix never sees an SVD, only
QRs and matmuls (the Chan pattern, typically faster than one large SVD on GPU). All mask-dependent
operations (\code{argsort}, gathers, diagonal indicators) are traced runtime data with static
shapes: one \code{jit} compilation covers every mask pattern (verified: a \code{lax.scan} over
heterogeneous masks traces its body once; the \code{jit} cache stays at size~1). A backend-agnostic
stable pads-last permutation needs no \code{kind=} argument: \code{argsort} of the integer key
$[\,i\text{ padded}\,]\cdot N + i$ has distinct keys. The bitwise-zero guarantees rest on
Householder semantics (true for LAPACK, cuSOLVER, and JAX's QR lowering on all backends) and hold
in \code{float32} unchanged --- zeros are exact in every format; only orthonormality and
$\sigma$-accuracy scale with precision.

\subsection{Integration into the T3 sweeps}\label{sec:t3}

In T3Toolbox the primitive replaces every SVD that produces \emph{kept basis columns} in the
uniform sweeps: the Tucker down-orthogonalization, both TT sweeps (as a pad-safe scan step whose
per-step masks ride the scan inputs), the down step, and the T3-SVD scan (unshared and shared).
Three integration points are worth recording:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Evolving masks.} The row mask of a mid-sweep unfolding is not an input mask: bond
ranks evolve by the sweep recurrences (e.g.\ $\ell_{j+1} = \min(\ell_j u_j,\, r_{j+1})$). All
evolved masks are precomputed on the host from the same recurrences the frame masks are built from,
and enter the compiled program as constants.
\item \textbf{A bitwise-clean chain.} Each site also \emph{zeroes its don't-care completion
columns} (they are real-supported but nonzero, and the next site's pad-safe SVD requires bitwise
zero padding). The remainder side is clean automatically: $s$ is exactly zero beyond $q$, so
pushed remainders have bitwise-zero padded rows. The whole sweep output is in canonical
clean-padding form for free.
\item \textbf{Gauge.} The uniform frame used to match the ragged frame \emph{bitwise} --- an
accident of both layers feeding the same matrices to the same LAPACK kernel, and impossible to
preserve: at a deficient point LAPACK's completion may live in the padded slots, which is the bug.
The pad-safe frame is gauge-\emph{equivalent}: same subspaces, same singular values, same
represented tensor, every gauge-invariant quantity (objectives, gradient norms, optimizer
trajectories) identical --- and at a deficient point even the completion \emph{subspaces}
legitimately differ, because the completion is a choice in both layers. Cross-representation
comparisons must be gauge-invariant (norms, projectors $U^{\top}U$, dense tensors) or transport
coordinates through the ambient embedding.
\end{itemize}

With the sweeps converted, the zero-padded continuation warm start keeps every tangent direction
(0 lost in all six regression cases, both frame paths, shared included), and the end-to-end uniform
continuation loop matches the ragged one.

\appendix
\section{Alternatives: designed, measured, rejected}\label{app:alt}

The path to Method~D went through four other designs plus two ``dumb'' baselines. Each contributed
a piece of the final algorithm; all are recorded here with what we measured. (Scripts:
\code{dev/review\_2026-08-22/repros/S1b/}.)

\subsection{Method A: two-sided augmentation, and the GSVD view}\label{app:A}

Pin the pad coordinates spectrally: with one-hot mask matrices $E_r, E_c$ and
$c = 4\norm{\Apad}_F$, take the SVD of
\[
\begin{bmatrix} \Apad & c\,E_r\\ c\,E_c^{\top} & 0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Each padded row/column forms an \emph{exact} singular triplet at $\sigma = c$, separated from the
data spectrum; every remaining singular vector --- including the $\sigma\approx 0$ ones --- is
orthogonal to the $c$-cluster, hence supported on real coordinates. Exact, tolerance-free, and the
origin of Step~2's core augmentation. Rejected as the \emph{outer} algorithm because its cost
grows with the pad count: the augmentation converts a row-pad count into extra \emph{columns}, so
with $P_r\sim N/2$ the QR costs $O(N^3)$ against $O(NM^2)$ unaugmented. Method~D applies the same
augmentation at the $M\times 2M$ core, where it costs $O(M^3)$.

\emph{The GSVD equivalence} (checked numerically, \code{s1b\_gsvd.py}): the augmentation is the
generalized SVD of the pair $(\!\Apad^{\top}, B)$ with $B^{\top}B = P_{\mathrm{real}}$
\cite{VanLoan1976,PaigeSaunders1981}. Because $P_{\mathrm{real}}\Apad = \Apad$ the pair
\emph{commutes}, and the generalized singular pairs sort the space into the data spectrum
(untouched), the real complement (the completion), and the common null space (the padding); the
augmented matrix $[\Apad \;\; \varepsilon B]$ has Gram
$\Apad\Apad^{\top} + \varepsilon^2 P_{\mathrm{real}}$ --- the same commuting pair folded into one
operator --- and LAPACK computes the GSVD as a QR-plus-CS decomposition of the stacked matrix,
which \emph{is} this augmentation. In exact arithmetic $\varepsilon$ affects only the ordering, so
there is no threshold. A thin variant for suffix-padded sites ($B=$ the first $n$ masked
coordinate vectors) was verified over 400 random cases at $O(Nn^2)$; Method~D subsumed it by
handling interior pads with the same machinery.

\subsection{Method B: post-hoc masked completion}\label{app:B}

Run the plain SVD; keep the $\sigma > \tau$ triplets (their left vectors are automatically clean,
$u = \Apad v/\sigma$); \emph{replace} the rest by explicitly constructed real-supported vectors: a
masked random block, orthogonalized against the kept columns (what TeNPy does by hand). Cost
$O(NM^2)$, independent of the pad count; exact for the kept vectors. Rejected because it
reintroduces a rank tolerance $\tau$ and a per-element number of replacements --- under \code{jit}
that is \code{where} / \code{take\_along\_axis} bookkeeping for a result Method~D produces with no
threshold at all. Method~D's Step~4 is exactly this completion realized through the permutation
rule instead of a threshold.

\subsection{Methods C, C$'$: masked noise, and Rayleigh--Ritz refinement}\label{app:C}

Add a tiny perturbation $\eta G$ supported on the real rows/columns and run the plain SVD: every
real direction gets $\sigma \gtrsim \eta > 0$ and outranks the padding, and the lifted vectors are
real-supported because $G$ is. The one real advantage: the matrix stays $N\times M$ (no extra
columns). The costs: the noisy Gram carries first-order cross terms $\Apad G^{\top} + G\Apad^{\top}$
--- the kept singular vectors rotate at $O(\eta/\mathrm{gap})$ where the augmentation perturbs at
second order (and not at all for the commuting $B$) --- values $\sigma\lesssim\eta$ are erased
($\eta$ is an implicit rank threshold), the balanced error is $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\norm{A}$ (the
Gram-route half-precision loss, and for the same reason), and random noise destroys determinism
(reproducibility, cache keys, cross-representation tests); a fixed dither fixes determinism and is
then exactly ``Method~A with the candidate block added into $\Apad$ instead of appended''. A
Rayleigh--Ritz pass on the clean matrix (C$'$) improves the balanced error to
$\varepsilon^{2/3}\norm{A}$ but cannot remove pad mass (a core-level rotation only redistributes
it). Kept in the record for one genuine niche: inside a differentiable pipeline the lifting also
regularizes the SVD derivative at repeated $\sigma$, which none of the exact methods do.

\subsection{Round-tripping through the ragged layer}\label{app:ragged}

Convert to ragged, run the per-element sweep (whose completions are real by construction), pack
back. Exact --- and not expressible under \code{jit}: the conversion produces data-dependent
shapes, the sweep is $C\cdot d$ small host-side kernels, and the compile-once loop breaks at every
frame rebuild. Viable only eagerly, where the uniform layer is already the slower path, so it never
competes where the problem actually lives.

\subsection{Shrinking the masks to the numerical rank}\label{app:shrink}

Treat the deficient point as a lower-rank point. Rejected outright: rank continuation exists
precisely to \emph{grow into} the padded directions; shrinking the masks removes the escape
directions the warm start is built to provide.

\subsection{The symmetric contract needs no runtime transpose}\label{app:sym}

The packet's original contract required $n\ge m$ (real rows $\ge$ real columns). Since real counts
are traced per-element data, dispatching on them would need \code{lax.cond} --- which under
batching executes \emph{both} branches --- and per-element transposition inside one batched linalg
call is not expressible at all. Measured resolution (\code{s1b\_sym\_variant.py}): the restriction
is a contract statement, not an algorithmic one. Zeroing the pinned $\sigma$'s \emph{by flag}
instead of by position (Step~3) is the only change needed; the same pipeline then serves any real
$(n,m)$ --- verified over 600 random cases spanning tall, wide, and statically-wide shapes with
interior pads, rank deficiency, and zero real rows and columns. The only transpose left is the
static one, a Python branch on concrete padded shapes.

\subsection{The core is blind: why prevention beats repair}\label{app:blind}

Take the $5\times 3$ walkthrough of the design record: rank-one data, interior pad rows, one
numerically zero \emph{real} row, entries chosen so the QR is exact in floating point. With the
permutation, $Q$'s null column lands on the zero \emph{real} row (allowed --- the requirement is
one-sided); without it, $H = I$ emits an exact pad coordinate --- total contamination,
deterministically. But in \emph{both} cases the projected core $B = Q^{\top}\Apad$ is bit-for-bit
\[
B = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 10 & 0\\ 0&0&0\\ 0&0&0\end{pmatrix}:
\]
a zero row of $B$ says only ``this column of $Q$ is orthogonal to $\col(\Apad)$'' --- it cannot
distinguish a clean null column from a poisoned one. The contamination lives entirely in $Q$,
upstream of anything the core sees, so no core-level repair is possible; the permutation rule at
$O(NM)$ is the only place the information exists.
